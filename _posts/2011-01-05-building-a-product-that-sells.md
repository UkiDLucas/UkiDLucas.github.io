Let's start with the product, because after all, that is what your customers will want and what will define all of your future efforts, regardless of how lofty your philosophy is.

## The better mousetrap myth

We need to start by dispatching the widely quoted statement "Build a better mousetrap and people will beat a path to your door."

On rare occasions it's true, especially for social networking apps like Facebook, or great stuff like Pandora. Most of the time, however, engineers create technological marvels that fail to catch the public's attention.

## Basic rules to follow

There is no single formula for success, but at least I wrote down a few characteristics of a successful application:

- performs the advertised function very well
- it's simple to use, according to the "Don't make me think" rule
- it's eye candy, designed for a specific demographic group
- it's well designed for the platform it runs on (phone, tablets)
- induces people to share stuff via social networks
- it's easy to find in app store search
- provides reasons to use it frequently
- reminds of itself via inoffensive notifications

First of all, your product should be useful. It does not matter if it is a game or a scientific calculator, but it should do what it is designed for very well.

The product should be very easy to use. Complicated options, confusing messaging, wrong assumptions, and functions that take too long to execute are a sure way to lose users.

The product should be designed well, taking into consideration your target demographic.

Your product should be designed in such a way as to encourage the user to share the experience, quotes, images, likes and comments via your product. The product might remind the user of new content available via notifications. Social sharing is the fastest and easiest way to reach a larger audience.

The product should be named well so people can find it. It should provide a good description, if possible in multiple languages.

## Build it for yourself

Finally, it is always a good idea to build a product that caters to your own needs, knowledge and interests.

People often fall into the trap of developing a product based on current fads and fashions. Development of a good product is usually a long process, and when you are not passionate about it, you probably will fall short of success.
