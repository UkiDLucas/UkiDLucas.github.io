Recently, I have been thinking about a new lever-action rifle.

The appeal of these late 1800s models is that they are great, very light (5 lbs) for backpacking, and pack enough punch for anything you need in the woods or bush.

Lever action rifles, compared to the heavy (~8.5 lbs) bolt action calibers (.30-06, 7.62x54R) we normally shoot, are far more maneuverable in the thicket. They are honestly safer with heavy animals like a boar, elk or moose, because of their 10+1 capacity and rapid fire ability.

![](http://www.americanrifleman.org/wp-content/uploads/Webcontent/images/2011-5/20115910292-4rifles_ms.jpg)

I have been thinking about the [caliber I would like to pick up](http://www.rossiusa.com/product-details.cfm?id=159), and here are the options I considered.

Please be aware that I reload my ammo, and I am not planning to pay $2 per round of [Buffalo Bore](https://www.buffalobore.com/index.php?l=search_list&s[title]=Y&s[short_desc]=Y&s[full_desc]=Y&s[sku]=Y&s[match]=all&s[cid]=0&s[search]=.454).

Also, I don't like revolvers, so there is no appeal for me in carrying a matching hand piece. I like my 1911 .45 ACP just fine for "personal" defense.

## .38 Special / .357 Magnum

While it would be a great caliber for fun plinking, smaller game and home defense, I decided that I need to rule it out, because it is definitely not a boar caliber, an important criterion for me.

## .44-40 Winchester

This is the grandpa of all lever-action cartridges, and while it is plenty powerful and honestly cool, it is not recommended as a boar load.

## .44 Special / .44 Magnum

This combination is easy to buy (or reload), with many options available (305gr.@1,325fps/1,189ft-lbs.).

The .44 Special is a great round for plinking and self defense. The .44 Magnum is designed for hunting, and it is a good boar load (deer and everything else smaller).

I have a feeling that this combination is the best for a lever action rifle.

## .45 Colt

Honestly, I wanted this one the most, especially since I already reload .45 ACP, which shares similar tooling.

The .45 Colt, while weak most of the time, can have very powerful +P options from [DoubleTap](http://www.midwayusa.com/product/769259/doubletap-ammunition-45-colt-long-colt-p-335-grain-wide-flat-nose-gas-check) (335 gr., 1605 fps, 1916 ft. lbs. in a 16 inch barrel).

## .454 Casull

A caliber that does all that the .44 and .45 Colt can, but on average has much more power (360gr.@1,425fps/1,623ft-lbs.). The cartridge is still SHORT enough in length, so you can carry many of them.

What is most important, you can shoot .45 Colt out of a .454 Casull chambered rifle. All you have to do is clean the chamber afterwards, to wipe the carbon deposits before you use a .454 round again.

I would have to make some extra-light loads (250 gr. & TrailBoss@1,100fps) for plinking, and I would have the LIGHT, MEDIUM and HEAVY rifle in one package.

This way I can shoot regular load .45 Colt for 80% of my needs, and have .454 for boar hunting and for the Yukon/Alaska trip I dream about.

## .45-70 Government

It is definitely plenty of gun (430gr.@1,925fps/3,537ft-lbs.) for ALL dangerous animals in the back country (boar, bear, moose, bison, mountain lion).

The problem with this round is that it is HUGE and takes up valuable space in the magazine tube of the rifle.

## .30-30

This is a great and proven lever action rifle cartridge, but similarly to the .45-70, I feel that it is too long and takes too much space.
